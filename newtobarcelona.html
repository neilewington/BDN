Visas & legal status

EU citizens

No visa needed. You can live and work freely. You should register on the Padrón municipal (local census) within 3 months and apply for your TIE (identity card) if staying long-term.

Non-EU: Digital Nomad Visa

Spain launched a Digital Nomad Visa in 2023. It allows remote workers and freelancers to live legally in Spain for up to 5 years. You'll need to show €2,160/month income (1x IPREM).

NIE number

You'll need a NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) for almost everything: opening a bank account, renting, signing contracts. Book an appointment at the Oficina de Extranjería.

Autónomo (self-employed)

If you're freelancing in Spain, you'll register as autónomo. It involves social security contributions and quarterly tax filings. Our partner Gestoria Sanz can help — see our Partners page.

BDN tip: The autónomo & visa process has a lot of nuance. Join our community and ask in the #legal channel — members share real experiences and can recommend trusted gestorías.

Finding housing

Short-term (first weeks)

Airbnb, Booking.com, or serviced apartments are easiest for your first 2–4 weeks while you get your bearings. Expect to pay €80–120/night in a decent area.

Medium-term (1–6 months)

Idealista, Habitaclia, and Facebook groups like "Barcelona Housing & Rooms" are your best bets. Furnished rooms in shared flats run €700–1,100/month depending on neighbourhood.

Long-term (6+ months)

A standard unfurnished rental requires a NIE, proof of income, and often 2 months' deposit. Average 1-bed in central Barcelona: €1,200–1,800/month.

Coliving

Coliving spaces like Ukio, Outsite, and Sende Barcelona include bills, wifi, and community events. Great for newcomers. Typically €1,200–2,000/month all-in.

BDN tip: Our community has a dedicated #housing channel where members post rooms, warn about scams, and share landlord recommendations. Join before you start your search.

Banking & money

Wise (recommended)

Most nomads use Wise as their primary account. Multi-currency, low fees, easy transfers. You can get a Spanish IBAN for direct debits. BDN members get a fee-free first transfer.

Revolut

Another popular option with a good app and free ATM withdrawals. Less useful for autónomos who need a Spanish bank account for tax purposes.

Spanish bank account

If you're registering as autónomo or signing a rental, you may need a local Spanish account. N26 (German but SEPA), CaixaBank, or Openbank are the easiest to open.

SIM card & internet

Best SIM options

Pepephone, Digi, and Simyo offer excellent value SIMs. Unlimited data plans from €10–20/month. Buy at a tobacconist (estanco), supermarket, or directly online.

Home internet

MásMóvil, Finetwork, and Digi offer competitive fibre plans from €20–30/month. Most apartments already have fibre infrastructure installed.

Coworking wifi

Barcelona has dozens of excellent coworking spaces with fast, reliable internet. Check our Partners page for BDN-discounted options.

Healthcare

EU citizens with EHIC

Your European Health Insurance Card gives you access to public healthcare. Once registered on the Padrón and with a TIE, you can register with a local health centre (CAP) for a GP.

Autónomos

When you register as autónomo, you automatically pay into the Spanish social security system and gain access to public healthcare.

Private insurance

Many nomads get private health insurance for faster access and English-speaking doctors. Sanitas, Cigna, and Adeslas are popular. BDN members get 15% off Sanitas — see Partners.

Neighbourhoods guide

Poblenou — Nomad central

The 22@ tech district. Full of coworking spaces, startups, and fellow nomads. Slightly cheaper than Eixample with a cool industrial feel. Great beach access.

Eixample — Central & convenient

The grid-plan heart of Barcelona. Excellent transport links, tons of cafés and restaurants, great for networking. The most expensive neighbourhood to rent in.

Gracia — Village vibes

A charming, bohemian neighbourhood with a village feel. Full of independent cafés and local restaurants. Very popular with creatives and freelancers.

El Born / Sant Pere — Trendy & walkable

Atmospheric medieval streets, great food scene, close to the beach. A bit touristy but fantastic to live in. Popular with younger nomads.

Getting around

Metro

Fast, frequent, and cheap. A T-Casual 10-journey card costs around €11. The metro covers the whole city and is the main way locals get around.

Cycling

Barcelona is one of Europe's most cycling-friendly cities. Bicing (monthly bike share from €50/year) or buy a second-hand bike on Wallapop.

Walking

Most central neighbourhoods are extremely walkable. You can walk from Barceloneta to Gracia in under 40 minutes through some of the best streets in Europe.
